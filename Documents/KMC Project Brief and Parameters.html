KMC dislocation glide in Ni-Fe

MSE454 • Working project brief • Update: November 23, 2026

Main question: How do Fe-related obstacles change dislocation velocity as temperature and applied shear stress vary?

Scope

Kinetic Monte Carlo (KMC) will generate every displacement and time increment. Start with independent effective segments gliding forward and backward in pure face-centered cubic (FCC) Ni, then add local barriers that depend on Fe concentration. The segments move along one slip direction and do not yet form a connected dislocation line.

Measure average velocity and plastic shear strain rate. Keep cross-slip, climb, precipitates, and full superalloy behavior outside the core scope.

KMC method

1. Set temperature, stress, composition, segment positions, and a random seed. For Ni-Fe, generate local obstacle barriers and keep them fixed during each run.

2. List the allowed forward and backward hops. Assign each event a barrier and a rate:

rᵢ = νᵢ exp[−ΔGᵢ / (k_B T)] • R = Σᵢ rᵢ

3. Choose event i with probability rᵢ/R. Advance time by Δt = −ln(u)/R, where u is an independent uniform random number in (0, 1).

4. Move the selected segment, update affected rates, and record position and time. Stop at the chosen event count or simulated duration.

5. Calculate v = Δx̄/Δt and γ̇ = ρbv. Repeat with different seeds and, for alloys, obstacle configurations. At fixed ρ, strain rate is a rescaled velocity.

Checks before interpreting the results

The current barrier law is E± = E₀ ∓ τV*. Convert τV* from joules to eV. The code floors the forward barrier at 10⁻⁴ eV, reached near 182 MPa; its 20 to 200 MPa sweep crosses this artificial limit. Revise the range or rate law before interpreting high-stress results.

Zhao et al. [3] study pure Ni and 50 at.% Fe using molecular dynamics. Their pure-Ni temperature trend differs from this baseline. Resolve the rate law and comparison regime within KMC before claiming quantitative agreement.

Check that c = 0 recovers the baseline and that forward/reverse rates obey detailed balance at zero stress. Alloy results should converge across run lengths and obstacle configurations.
